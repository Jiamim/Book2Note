Tiny tyrants: How to really change your kid's behavior

Source: Kazdin, A.E., with Carlo Rotella, (2008, April 10). Tiny tyrants: How to really change your kid's behavior. Slate Magazine, http://www.slate.com/id/2188744 /

Summary: This article reiterates the information found in the Postive Opposite video. The article reads, “The best way to get rid of unwanted behavior is to train a desirable one to replace it. So turn "I want him to stop having tantrums" into "I want him to stay calm and not to raise his voice when I say no to him."”

If you’re good, I’ll buy you a toy

Source: Kazdin, A.E., & Rotella, C. (2010, March 26). If you’re good, I’ll buy you a toy: The difference between bribing your child and rewarding your child. Slate Magazine, http://www.slate.com/id/2248766

Summary: This article challenges several common objections to using rewards for your child. Rewards work if implemented clealy and simply. “Rewards tend to become bribery when they're unsystematic, unconnected to clear parental expectations, and used to try to get your kids to do things at odds with the model you provide with your own behavior.” This article also touches on lessons learned in previous videos such as Praise and Shaping.

The messy room dilemma

Source: Kazdin, A.E., & Rotella, C. (2009, March 27). The messy room dilemma: When to ignore behavior, when to change it. Slate Magazine, http://www.slate.com/id/2214678/

Summary: This article puts into question why you want certain behaviors changed. Can you let it go? Can you comprise? Can you ignore it all together? Planned ignoring may seem counter-intuitive, but the lack of attention to a negative behavior might be the remedy you are looking for!
